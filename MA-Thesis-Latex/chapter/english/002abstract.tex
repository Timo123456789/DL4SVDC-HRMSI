\section*{Abstract}
The precise detection and classification of small vehicles in high-resolution remote sensing data is essential for applications such as traffic monitoring and urban planning.
The aim of this study was to investigate the influence of additional spectral channels on object detection performance.
Experiments on multispectral aerial imagery show that the inclusion of an infrared (IR) channel improves the detection accuracy of small vehicles, while vegetation indices such as \acrshort{NDVI} are not consistently beneficial. \acrshort{RGB} data alone already provides solid results, with the availability of extensive, high-quality training data having a greater impact on model performance than adding spectral information or using a more recent model version. \acrshort{YOLO}v9 proves to be particularly well suited for detecting and classifying small objects.
The results confirm the potential of \Acrlong{DL} methods for vehicle detection and classification in multispectral remote sensing data and open up promising directions for future research, such as the use of multitemporal data or the analysis of scaling effects.
