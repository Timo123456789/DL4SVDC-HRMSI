\chapter{Conclusion and Outlook}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

The research questions formulated in this study were successfully addressed. 
The results indicate that incorporating additional image channels, such as infrared (IR), can enhance detection performance—particularly for small objects—whereas spectral indices like NDVI do not necessarily yield improvements. 
The experiments demonstrate that \acrshort{RGB} data alone provides a robust foundation for detecting small vehicles, although the inclusion of \acrshort{IR} data further increases accuracy. Moreover, the findings reveal that the availability of extensive, high-quality training data exerts a greater influence on model performance than either expanding the input channels with additional spectral information or employing a more recent model version. 
Finally, the experimental results substantiate that \acrshort{YOLO}v9 is particularly effective for small object detection and classification tasks, and that its architectural advancements relative to earlier iterations, such as \acrshort{YOLO}v3, lead to statistically significant improvements in detection accuracy.

\section{Outlook}
Future work should evaluate the systematic integration of additional spectral indices and channels to more accurately determine their influence on model performance.
Furthermore, the application and modification of additional \acrshort{YOLO} variants and comparison with Two-Stage detectors (e.g., \acrshort{R-CNN}-based methods) offer a promising field of investigation.
Another approach is to analyse scaling effects, for example by reducing the spatial resolution to satellite level, in order to evaluate the transferability to raw data from real systems (e.g. Airbus, 30 cm/pixel \cite{airbus_neo}).
The role of object-specific features, such as vehicle colour, should also be investigated in greater depth.
In addition, assigning scene classification labels (e.g., desert, glacier, forest, urban) to each image within the training dataset could help emphasise specific focus areas during model training or ensure a more balanced distribution across different scene types.
In the long term, the use of multitemporal data in particular opens up new perspectives, as it allows dynamic patterns and changes in the space-time context (e.g. commuter traffic versus long-term parking) to be captured.
